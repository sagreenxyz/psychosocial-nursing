## What Is Therapeutic Communication?

**Therapeutic communication** is a purposeful, planned, and deliberate use of verbal and non-verbal communication to promote patient health and healing. Unlike social conversation, therapeutic communication is guided by clinical goals: helping patients explore feelings, gain insight, problem-solve, and feel understood.

The American Nurses Association identifies communication as a core nursing competency. When done well, it builds trust, reduces anxiety, improves health literacy, and supports emotional recovery.

## Core Principles of Therapeutic Communication

### 1. Be Patient-Centered
Every interaction should center on the patient's needs, not the nurse's agenda or comfort. Ask yourself: *Who is this conversation serving?*

### 2. Use Active Empathy
Empathy is not sympathy. It means striving to understand the patient's experience from *their* perspective — acknowledging their feelings without judgment.

> **Empathy:** "It sounds like you're feeling overwhelmed by all of this."
> **Sympathy:** "Oh, that's so sad. I feel so sorry for you."

### 3. Maintain Non-Judgmental Stance
Patients must feel safe to disclose sensitive information — about substance use, mental health, sexual behavior, or trauma — without fear of judgment.

### 4. Use Purposeful Silence
Silence is a powerful therapeutic tool. Allowing pauses gives patients time to process emotions and formulate responses.

### 5. Be Genuine (Authenticity)
Patients quickly detect inauthentic interactions. Self-congruence (alignment between what you say and how you say it) builds trust.

### 6. Maintain Confidentiality (Within Limits)
Patients must know that what they share remains private — except in specific circumstances. Clearly explain the limits of confidentiality early in the relationship.

## Therapeutic Communication Techniques

| Technique | Description | Example |
|-----------|-------------|---------|
| **Open-ended questions** | Invite elaboration | "Tell me more about how you've been feeling." |
| **Reflection** | Mirror back feelings | "It sounds like you're really frustrated." |
| **Clarification** | Seek to understand better | "When you say 'overwhelmed,' what does that look like for you?" |
| **Restatement** | Repeat key words or ideas | Patient: "I just can't take it anymore." Nurse: "You can't take it anymore." |
| **Summarizing** | Synthesize what was shared | "So over the past week, you've noticed..." |
| **Focusing** | Guide back to a central theme | "I notice we keep coming back to your relationship with your daughter." |
| **Informing** | Share relevant information clearly | "This medication may cause drowsiness for the first week." |
| **Offering self** | Express availability | "I have time to sit with you right now if you'd like." |
| **Therapeutic silence** | Allow pauses | *[Sitting quietly with the patient]* |

## Non-Therapeutic Communication — Patterns to Avoid

| Non-Therapeutic Pattern | Why It's Harmful | Example |
|------------------------|------------------|---------|
| **False reassurance** | Dismisses real concerns | "Everything's going to be fine!" |
| **Giving advice** | Removes patient autonomy | "You should just leave him." |
| **Changing the subject** | Blocks emotional expression | Patient talks about fear; nurse asks about medications |
| **Probing/interrogating** | Creates defensiveness | "Why did you do that? Why didn't you call your doctor?" |
| **Minimizing** | Invalidates experience | "Lots of people have it worse." |
| **Using clichés** | Feels hollow and dismissive | "Time heals all wounds." |

## Putting It Together: A Sample Interaction

**Non-Therapeutic Version:**
> Patient: "I've been feeling really down. I don't see the point in any of this treatment."
> Nurse: "Oh, don't worry! Once you start feeling better physically, your mood will improve too. Now let me check your vitals."

**Therapeutic Version:**
> Patient: "I've been feeling really down. I don't see the point in any of this treatment."
> Nurse: *(pauses, makes eye contact)* "It sounds like you're really struggling right now — like the treatment feels pointless." *(waits)*
> Patient: "Yeah. Every time I come in, something new is wrong."
> Nurse: "That sounds exhausting. Can you tell me more about what's been hardest?"

---

## Key Takeaways

- Therapeutic communication is purposeful, goal-directed, and patient-centered
- Key principles include empathy, authenticity, non-judgment, and appropriate use of silence
- Use specific techniques (open-ended questions, reflection, clarification) consistently
- Avoid common non-therapeutic patterns that block or dismiss patients' emotional expression
