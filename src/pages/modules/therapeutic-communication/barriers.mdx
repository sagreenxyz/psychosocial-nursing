## Understanding Communication Barriers

Even with the best intentions, therapeutic communication can be blocked by a wide range of factors. Recognizing these barriers is the first step toward overcoming them.

---

## Patient-Related Barriers

### Cognitive and Perceptual Challenges
- **Dementia or delirium:** Impaired memory, disorientation, and confusion impede information exchange
- **Psychosis:** Hallucinations or delusions may distort reality
- **Intellectual disability:** May require adapted communication and visual aids
- **Low health literacy:** ~36% of U.S. adults have basic or below-basic health literacy (NAAL, 2006)

### Emotional Barriers
- **Fear and anxiety:** Patients may conceal concerns to avoid "bad news"
- **Shame and stigma:** Mental health symptoms often underreported due to fear of judgment
- **Grief and shock:** Recent diagnosis or loss may impair information processing
- **Anger:** Patients who feel unheard may direct frustration toward nurses

### Language and Literacy Barriers
- Non-English-speaking patients are entitled to professional interpretation services
- Using family members as interpreters creates ethical concerns (role confusion, privacy breaches)
- Plain language (6th grade reading level) should be used for all patient education

---

## Nurse-Related Barriers

### Communication Style Issues
- **Jargon:** Medical terminology alienates patients who don't understand it
- **Leading questions:** "You've been taking your medication every day, right?"
- **Excessive questioning:** Rapid-fire questions feel like interrogation
- **Advice-giving:** Telling patients what to do undermines autonomy and trust

### Emotional and Cognitive Barriers
- **Burnout and compassion fatigue:** Depleted nurses struggle to be fully present
- **Personal bias and prejudice:** Unconscious associations affect tone and assumptions
- **Countertransference:** Unprocessed reactions to patients impair therapeutic effectiveness
- **Task focus:** When nurses focus on completing tasks, they miss patients' emotional cues

---

## Environmental Barriers

| Environment | Challenge | Strategy |
|-------------|-----------|----------|
| Shared rooms | Lack of privacy | Use curtains, lower your voice, identify private spaces |
| Busy units | Frequent interruptions | Schedule dedicated conversation time |
| Noise | Impairs hearing and concentration | Find quieter locations; face the patient directly |
| Time constraints | Rushed interactions | Acknowledge time: "I have 10 minutes. Let's use them well." |
| Technology | EHR-focused interactions | Step away from the screen during sensitive conversations |

---

## Stigma as a Barrier

**Mental health stigma** remains a pervasive barrier. Patients with psychiatric diagnoses often report being dismissed, having symptoms attributed to mental illness ("psychiatric shortcut"), and being treated with less dignity.

**Nurses can counter stigma by:**
- Using person-first language ("person with schizophrenia" not "schizophrenic")
- Treating mental health concerns with the same seriousness as physical complaints
- Examining their own beliefs and language about mental illness
- Advocating for equitable care within their teams

---

## Adapting Communication for Special Populations

| Population | Adaptation |
|-----------|------------|
| **Deaf/hard of hearing** | Ensure hearing aids; use interpreter; face patient directly |
| **Non-English speaking** | Arrange professional interpreter (in person or via telephone/video) |
| **Pediatric patients** | Use developmentally appropriate language; include family when appropriate |
| **Older adults with cognitive impairment** | Simple sentences, allow extra time, reduce distractions |
| **Patients with aphasia** | Use simple yes/no questions, picture boards, writing; be patient |
| **Patients in psychosis** | Stay calm, clear, concrete; avoid arguing with delusions |

---

## Key Takeaways

- Communication barriers arise from the patient, the nurse, and the environment — all can be addressed
- Stigma toward mental illness is a significant systemic barrier to therapeutic communication
- Adapting communication style to each patient's needs is an essential competency
- Time constraints are real, but brief, focused, therapeutic interactions are always possible

3. A patient's family member offers to interpret. What are the ethical concerns, and what should you do instead?
