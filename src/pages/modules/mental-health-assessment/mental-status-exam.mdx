## What Is the Mental Status Examination?

The **Mental Status Examination (MSE)** is the psychological equivalent of a physical examination. It is a systematic, structured assessment of a patient's current mental functioning conducted through observation and direct questioning.

The MSE is:
- A **snapshot in time** — capturing the patient's mental state at the moment of assessment
- A **clinical communication tool** — allowing standardized documentation and handoff
- A **screening and monitoring instrument** — identifying change over time or from baseline

---

## Domains of the Mental Status Examination

### 1. Appearance
**Observe:** Grooming and hygiene, dress, age vs. stated age, nutritional status

*Example documentation: "Patient appears approximately stated age, casually dressed, with poor hygiene and unkempt hair. Clothing is mismatched and weather-inappropriate."*

### 2. Behavior and Psychomotor Activity
**Observe:** Level of activity (calm, agitated, psychomotor retardation), eye contact, gait and posture, involuntary movements

### 3. Attitude
**Descriptors:** cooperative, guarded, suspicious, hostile, evasive, seductive, demanding, apathetic, engaged

### 4. Speech

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Pressured | Rapid, difficult to interrupt |
| Poverty of speech | Sparse responses |
| Dysarthria | Slurred articulation |
| Latency | Prolonged pause before responding |

### 5. Mood and Affect

**Mood** = Patient's subjective emotional state (use direct quotation): *"Patient reports mood as 'okay, I guess.'"*

**Affect** = Clinician's observation of emotional expression:

| Affect Quality | Descriptor |
|----------------|------------|
| Range | Full, restricted, blunted, flat |
| Appropriateness | Congruent or incongruent with mood/content |
| Mobility | Labile (rapidly shifting) vs. fixed |

### 6. Thought Process
The *form* or pattern of thinking:

| Term | Definition |
|------|-----------|
| **Logical/linear** | Organized, goal-directed — normal |
| **Circumstantial** | Gets to the point eventually, with many tangents |
| **Tangential** | Never gets to the point |
| **Flight of ideas** | Rapid topic shifts with loose connections |
| **Loose associations** | Illogical connections between ideas |
| **Thought blocking** | Abrupt interruption mid-thought |

### 7. Thought Content
**Assess for:** Delusions, obsessions, phobias, suicidal ideation, homicidal ideation, paranoid ideation

### 8. Perceptual Disturbances

| Type | Example |
|------|---------|
| Hallucinations (auditory) | Hearing voices when no one is speaking |
| Hallucinations (visual) | Seeing things others don't see |
| Illusions | Misinterpreting real stimuli |
| Depersonalization | Feeling detached from oneself |

### 9. Cognition

**Standard tests:**
- **Orientation:** Person, place, time, situation
- **Memory:** Immediate, short-term (recall 3 words at 5 min), long-term
- **Concentration:** Serial 7s (100-7-7...) or spell "WORLD" backwards
- **Abstract thinking:** Proverb interpretation

> For structured cognitive screening, use validated tools: **MMSE**, **MoCA**, or **Mini-Cog**.

### 10. Insight and Judgment

**Insight:** Does the patient understand that they have a mental health condition?
- None / Minimal / Partial / Full

**Judgment:** Can the patient make reasonable decisions?
- Ask hypothetical: "If you smelled smoke in the hospital, what would you do?"

## Sample MSE Documentation

> *"44-year-old male, appears stated age, disheveled with poor hygiene. Eye contact variable. Cooperative but guarded. Speech is normal rate and volume. Mood reported as 'angry.' Affect is congruent, restricted in range. Thought process is logical. Thought content notable for paranoid ideation (believes staff are 'recording him'). Denies auditory hallucinations. Oriented ×3. Memory grossly intact. Insight is minimal. Judgment is impaired."*

---

## Key Takeaways

- The MSE is a structured, systematic snapshot of current mental functioning
- Each domain has specific terminology — use it consistently for communication clarity
- Document using objective, behavioral language — avoid interpretive or pejorative labels
- The MSE complements, but does not replace, the psychosocial history and risk assessment
