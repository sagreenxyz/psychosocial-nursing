## What Is Complicated Grief?

**Complicated grief** — now formally recognized in the DSM-5-TR as **Prolonged Grief Disorder (PGD)** — is a clinically significant condition where grief fails to follow the typical adaptive trajectory and instead becomes persistently debilitating.

### DSM-5-TR Criteria for Prolonged Grief Disorder
- The loss occurred **≥12 months ago** (adults; 6 months in children)
- **Intense yearning/longing** for the deceased
- **Preoccupation** with thoughts or memories of the deceased
- **≥3 of 8 symptoms** present most days at clinically significant intensity:
  - Identity disruption
  - Disbelief about the death
  - Avoidance of reminders of the loss
  - Intense emotional pain
  - Difficulty re-engaging with life
  - Emotional numbness
  - Feeling life is meaningless without the deceased
  - Intense loneliness

---

## Distinguishing Complicated Grief from Depression and PTSD

| Feature | Complicated Grief | Major Depression | PTSD |
|---------|------------------|-----------------|------|
| Core focus | Longing for the specific person lost | Generalized low mood, anhedonia | Fear/threat response |
| Triggers | Reminders of the deceased | Broad range | Trauma reminders |
| Response to antidepressants | Limited | Generally good | Partial |

> Complicated grief often *co-occurs* with depression and PTSD — screening for all three is recommended.

---

## Risk Factors for Complicated Grief

| Risk Factor | Example |
|-------------|---------|
| **Nature of the loss** | Sudden, traumatic, or violent death; suicide loss; death of a child |
| **Relationship to deceased** | Highly dependent relationships; ambivalent or conflicted relationships |
| **Prior history** | Previous losses, depression, anxiety, or trauma |
| **Social factors** | Social isolation, lack of support, stigmatized loss |

## Evidence-Based Interventions

### Complicated Grief Treatment (CGT)
CGT (Shear et al.) is the gold-standard psychotherapy for PGD. Components include:
- Loss-focused work (exploring the story of the death, processing avoided emotions)
- Restoration-focused work (life goals, reconnecting with others, future planning)
- Imaginal revisiting of the loss (graded exposure)

### Nursing Role in Complicated Grief
- **Screen** using validated tools at appropriate intervals
- **Normalize** the diagnosis — many patients feel shame about "not getting over it"
- **Psychoeducate** — Explain that PGD is a recognized, treatable condition
- **Refer** to grief-specialized counselors or therapists trained in CGT
- **Address co-occurring conditions** — Treat depression or PTSD as indicated

---

## Suicide Risk in Complicated Grief

Individuals with complicated grief have elevated rates of:
- Suicidal ideation and attempts
- Functional impairment
- Physical health decline

> **Always screen for suicidal ideation** when assessing for complicated grief.

---

## Key Takeaways

- Prolonged Grief Disorder (PGD) is a DSM-5-TR recognized condition with specific diagnostic criteria
- It is distinct from — but often co-occurs with — depression and PTSD
- Risk is highest following traumatic, sudden, or stigmatized losses
- Evidence-based treatment (CGT) and nursing support significantly improve outcomes
