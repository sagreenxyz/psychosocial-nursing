## Defining Key Terms

| Term | Definition |
|------|-----------|
| **Loss** | The experience of being deprived of something or someone of value |
| **Grief** | The internal, emotional response to loss |
| **Mourning** | The outward, culturally shaped expression of grief |
| **Bereavement** | The period of adjustment following a significant loss |
| **Anticipatory grief** | Grieving that occurs *before* an expected loss (e.g., terminal diagnosis) |

---

## Types of Loss

Patients experience many forms of loss beyond death:
- Loss of **health or function** (chronic illness, disability, amputation)
- Loss of **identity** (job loss, retirement, role changes)
- Loss of **relationships** (divorce, estrangement, death)
- Loss of **safety** (trauma, violence, displacement)
- Loss of **future** (infertility, terminal diagnosis)

> Nurses encounter loss in almost every clinical encounter. Recognizing the breadth of loss — not only death — enables more compassionate and holistic care.

## Kübler-Ross Stage Model (1969)

Elisabeth Kübler-Ross's landmark work *On Death and Dying* described five stages of grief:

1. **Denial** — "This can't be happening."
2. **Anger** — "Why me? This isn't fair!"
3. **Bargaining** — "If only I had gone to the doctor sooner..."
4. **Depression** — Profound sadness and withdrawal
5. **Acceptance** — Coming to terms with the reality of loss

> **Important limitation:** These stages are not linear, universal, or predictable. Patients move through them in any order, skip stages, or return to earlier stages. Do not tell patients where they "should" be in their grief.

---

## Worden's Task Model (1982/2009)

William Worden proposed a **task model** that emphasizes active engagement in grief:

| Task | Description |
|------|-------------|
| **1. Accept the reality of the loss** | Moving from intellectual acknowledgment to emotional acceptance |
| **2. Work through the pain of grief** | Allowing oneself to feel and process painful emotions |
| **3. Adjust to a world without the deceased** | Developing new roles, skills, and sense of self |
| **4. Maintain a connection with the deceased while embarking on a new life** | Transforming the relationship — not "letting go" but integrating the loss |

## Normal Grief Responses

| Domain | Common Manifestations |
|--------|-----------------------|
| **Physical** | Fatigue, insomnia, appetite changes, chest tightness, immune suppression |
| **Emotional** | Sadness, anger, guilt, anxiety, loneliness, relief, numbness |
| **Cognitive** | Disbelief, difficulty concentrating, preoccupation with thoughts of the deceased |
| **Behavioral** | Crying, withdrawal, restlessness, dreaming of deceased |

---

## Nursing Interventions for Grief

1. **Be present** — Simply sitting with a grieving patient can be powerfully therapeutic
2. **Validate the grief** — "There's no right or wrong way to grieve."
3. **Avoid minimizing** — Do not say "at least they didn't suffer" or "time heals all wounds"
4. **Facilitate expression** — "Would it help to talk about what he was like?"
5. **Provide psychoeducation** — Normalize grief responses; distinguish normal grief from depression
6. **Assess for complicated grief** — Monitor for prolonged, intense, or impairing grief
7. **Refer** — Bereavement counselors, grief support groups, spiritual care

---

## Key Takeaways

- Grief encompasses physical, emotional, cognitive, and behavioral responses to loss
- Kübler-Ross describes grief in stages (non-linear); Worden describes grief in tasks (active)
- Normal grief is wide-ranging; nurses should normalize diverse expressions
- Presence, validation, and facilitation are the core nursing interventions for acute grief
