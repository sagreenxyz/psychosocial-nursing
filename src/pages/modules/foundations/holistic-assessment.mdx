## What Is a Holistic Assessment?

A **holistic assessment** looks beyond the chief complaint to understand the patient as a whole person — with a unique history, emotional life, social network, cultural background, and spiritual beliefs. This comprehensive view enables nurses to:

- Identify underlying contributing factors to illness
- Detect early signs of psychological distress
- Design individualized, culturally responsive care plans
- Establish rapport and trust with the patient

---

## Domains of Holistic Assessment

### 1. Biological Domain
- Medical and surgical history
- Current medications and allergies
- Physical examination findings
- Nutrition, sleep, exercise, and substance use

### 2. Psychological Domain
- Mood and affect
- Anxiety, depression, or trauma history
- Coping mechanisms and resilience
- Cognitive functioning and insight
- Mental health diagnoses and treatment history

### 3. Social Domain
- Living situation and relationships
- Social support network
- Occupational and financial status
- History of trauma, abuse, or violence
- Social determinants of health (housing, food security, transportation)

### 4. Spiritual Domain
- Religious and spiritual beliefs and practices
- Sources of meaning, hope, and purpose
- Spiritual distress or conflict
- Cultural health beliefs and practices

---

## The HOPE Framework for Spiritual Assessment

The **HOPE** framework (Anandarajah & Hight, 2001) provides a structured approach to eliciting spiritual and existential information in a clinical context:

| Letter | Concept | Example Questions |
|--------|---------|-------------------|
| **H** | Sources of **Hope** | "What gives you hope or strength right now?" |
| **O** | **Organized** religion | "Do you have a religious or spiritual community that supports you?" |
| **P** | **Personal** spiritual practices | "Do you have personal practices like prayer, meditation, or other rituals?" |
| **E** | **Effects** on medical care | "Are there beliefs or practices I should know about that affect your care?" |

## Psychosocial Screening Tools

Several validated tools support psychosocial assessment in clinical settings:

| Tool | Purpose |
|------|---------|
| **PHQ-9** | Screens for depression severity (9 items) |
| **GAD-7** | Screens for generalized anxiety disorder |
| **AUDIT-C** | Alcohol use disorder screening (3 items) |
| **DAST-10** | Drug abuse screening tool |
| **CAGE** | Brief alcohol use assessment (4 items) |
| **EPDS** | Edinburgh Postnatal Depression Scale |
| **PC-PTSD-5** | Primary Care PTSD screen (5 items) |

> **Clinical Note:** Positive screens require follow-up clinical assessment — they are not diagnostic but indicate the need for further evaluation.

---

## Conducting the Psychosocial Interview

Effective psychosocial assessment requires more than using a checklist. Key communication strategies include:

- **Create privacy** — Find a quiet, confidential space whenever possible
- **Use open-ended questions** — "How have you been feeling emotionally lately?"
- **Listen actively** — Reflect feelings, paraphrase, avoid interrupting
- **Normalize** — "Many people going through treatment feel anxious. How are you handling the stress?"
- **Ask sensitively about trauma** — "Sometimes past experiences affect how we feel today. Is there anything from your past that feels relevant to share?"

## Case Example: Holistic Assessment in Practice

**Patient:** James, 34-year-old male, admitted with asthma exacerbation.

**Standard charting notes:**
- Asthma since childhood, poorly controlled
- Uses rescue inhaler >2× per week
- Recent ED visit

**Holistic assessment reveals:**
- **Psychological:** Reports high stress, "can't sleep," describes mood as "flat"
- **Social:** Recently laid off, living with aging parents, strained relationship with girlfriend
- **Spiritual:** Previously found comfort in church but has "stopped going" since job loss

**Nursing implications:**
- Screen for depression (PHQ-9)
- Assess for environmental asthma triggers in home (e.g., mold, pets, smoking)
- Refer to social work for employment resources and insurance enrollment
- Explore spiritual/community reconnection as a source of support

---

## Key Takeaways

- Holistic assessment addresses biological, psychological, social, and spiritual domains
- Validated screening tools (PHQ-9, GAD-7, AUDIT-C) support systematic psychosocial assessment
- Effective psychosocial interviewing requires privacy, active listening, and sensitive language
- A comprehensive assessment informs individualized, patient-centered care plans
