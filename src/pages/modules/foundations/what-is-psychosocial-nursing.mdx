## Overview

Psychosocial nursing integrates the **psychological** and **social** dimensions of human experience into nursing care. While traditional biomedical models focus primarily on physical disease processes, psychosocial nursing recognizes that mental well-being, emotional states, relationships, cultural context, and social circumstances profoundly influence health outcomes.

Nurses are ideally positioned to address these dimensions: they spend more time with patients than any other healthcare professional, building relationships that enable them to recognize distress, offer support, and connect patients with appropriate resources.

---

## The Biopsychosocial Model

The **biopsychosocial model**, introduced by physician George Engel in 1977, proposed that health and illness are shaped by three interacting domains:

| Domain | Examples |
|--------|----------|
| **Biological** | Genetics, physiology, medications, chronic disease |
| **Psychological** | Emotions, coping styles, cognitive patterns, mental health diagnoses |
| **Social** | Family relationships, socioeconomic status, cultural beliefs, social support |

This model replaced the purely biomedical view of illness and provided a theoretical basis for holistic, patient-centered nursing care.

> **Key Point:** A patient with diabetes who is also experiencing depression, social isolation, and financial insecurity cannot be effectively cared for by treating only their blood glucose levels.

---

## Why Psychosocial Nursing Matters

Research consistently shows that psychosocial factors significantly influence:

- **Treatment adherence** — Patients with depression are 3× less likely to adhere to medical regimens (DiMatteo et al., 2000)
- **Recovery trajectories** — Strong social support is associated with better outcomes across most chronic conditions
- **Health disparities** — Social determinants such as poverty, racism, and lack of housing are leading drivers of health inequality
- **Patient satisfaction** — Emotional support and therapeutic communication are among the strongest predictors of patient satisfaction

---

## The Scope of Psychosocial Nursing Practice

Psychosocial nursing spans all care settings and patient populations. Whether in:

- **Acute care** — Supporting patients and families through diagnosis, surgical procedures, or critical illness
- **Primary care** — Screening for depression, anxiety, and substance use; addressing health literacy
- **Community/public health** — Connecting underserved populations with mental health and social resources
- **Psychiatric settings** — Providing specialized care for patients with serious mental illness

...nurses serve as advocates, therapeutic communicators, educators, and care coordinators.

## Case Study: Mrs. Rivera

**Scenario:** Mrs. Rivera, a 58-year-old Latina woman, was admitted for poorly controlled hypertension. She reports taking her medications inconsistently. When you ask about her home situation, she tearfully discloses that her husband recently passed away, she is living alone for the first time, and she is struggling with what she describes as "sadness all the time" and not eating well.

**Psychosocial assessment reveals:**
- Bereavement (potential complicated grief)
- Social isolation
- Possible depression
- Poor nutrition
- Medication non-adherence secondary to depression and social disruption

**Nursing implications:**
A nurse focused solely on blood pressure education would miss the underlying psychosocial drivers of this patient's health crisis. Effective care requires addressing grief, social support, depression screening, and connecting Mrs. Rivera with community resources.

---

## Key Concepts to Remember

- **Psychosocial nursing** addresses the emotional, mental, and social aspects of patient care alongside physical needs
- The **biopsychosocial model** provides the theoretical framework for holistic assessment
- Social determinants of health — including housing, income, education, and social support — are among the most powerful influences on health outcomes
- Nurses are uniquely positioned to identify and address psychosocial needs through therapeutic relationships
