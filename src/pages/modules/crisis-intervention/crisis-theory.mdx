## What Is a Crisis?

A **crisis** is a period of psychological disequilibrium resulting from a precipitating event that the individual perceives as threatening and cannot resolve with their usual coping strategies. It is not the event itself, but the person's **perception** and **inability to cope** that defines a crisis.

Key characteristics:
- **Time-limited:** Crises are acute, typically resolving within 4–6 weeks
- **Perception-dependent:** The same event may be crisisogenic for one person but not another
- **Opportunity:** Crisis presents both danger and opportunity for growth

---

## Types of Crises

### Developmental (Maturational) Crises
Predictable life transitions that create temporary disequilibrium:
- Adolescence and identity formation
- Marriage or partnership
- Parenthood
- Retirement, death of parents

### Situational Crises
Unexpected events that overwhelm coping capacity:
- Sudden illness or injury (self or loved one)
- Natural disaster
- Job loss or financial collapse
- Divorce or relationship breakup
- Traumatic assault

### Existential Crises
Challenges to fundamental questions of meaning, purpose, and identity:
- Terminal diagnosis
- Loss of faith or worldview
- Moral injury (especially in healthcare workers)

## Crisis Theory: Key Theorists

| Theorist | Contribution |
|----------|-------------|
| **Erich Lindemann (1944)** | Identified stages of grief after the Cocoanut Grove fire — foundational for crisis theory |
| **Gerald Caplan (1964)** | Defined crisis as time-limited; proposed four phases of crisis response |
| **Donna Aguilera (1998)** | Developed the Crisis Paradigm Model — balancing factors determine resolution |

### Aguilera's Balancing Factors
Crisis resolution depends on three key factors:
1. **Realistic perception of the event** — Does the person understand what actually happened?
2. **Adequate situational support** — Does the person have people to turn to?
3. **Adequate coping mechanisms** — Does the person have effective coping strategies?

## The ABC Model of Crisis Intervention

| Letter | Component | Core Question |
|--------|-----------|---------------|
| **A** | Achieve contact | "Am I connecting with this person?" |
| **B** | Boil down the problem | "What is the core issue?" |
| **C** | Cope | "What can we do right now to help this person cope?" |

---

## Roberts' Seven-Stage Crisis Intervention Model

1. **Assess lethality** — Is the person safe right now?
2. **Establish rapport and relationship** — Build connection quickly
3. **Identify the major problems** — What precipitated the crisis?
4. **Deal with feelings and emotions** — Validate and encourage expression
5. **Generate and explore alternatives** — What options exist?
6. **Develop an action plan** — Concrete, achievable steps
7. **Follow-up** — Check in; ensure safety and progress

## Case Application: Roberts' Model in Action

**Scenario:** Amara, 29, arrives at the ED after her partner of 5 years ended their relationship. She reports "can't stop crying," inability to eat or sleep for 3 days, and passive suicidal ideation ("I don't want to be here anymore, but I wouldn't do anything").

| Stage | Nursing Response |
|-------|----------------|
| Assess lethality | Screen using C-SSRS — passive ideation, no plan, no intent confirmed |
| Establish rapport | "I can see you're really hurting. I'm here. Tell me what's been happening." |
| Identify problems | Relationship loss, acute grief, sleep deprivation, social isolation |
| Deal with feelings | Validate: "Three days without sleep after that kind of loss — of course you're overwhelmed." |
| Generate alternatives | Identify supportive friends, therapist referral, crisis line number |
| Action plan | Contact a friend tonight, schedule therapy appointment, 988 saved in phone |
| Follow-up | Schedule 48-hour phone check-in; coordinate outpatient social work referral |

---

## Key Takeaways

- Crisis is defined by the individual's perception and inability to cope — not the event itself
- Crises are time-limited and present opportunities for growth alongside danger
- Multiple theoretical models (ABC, Roberts) provide structured approaches to crisis intervention
- Lethality assessment is always the first priority in any crisis situation
