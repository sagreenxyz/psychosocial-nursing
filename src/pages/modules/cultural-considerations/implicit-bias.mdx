## What Is Implicit Bias?

**Implicit bias** refers to attitudes and stereotypes that operate unconsciously — influencing judgments and behavior without our awareness or intention. Unlike explicit bias (deliberate prejudice), implicit bias affects even individuals who consciously value fairness and equality.

The **Implicit Association Test (IAT)**, developed at Harvard, has demonstrated that implicit racial, gender, weight, age, and disability biases are widespread — including among healthcare providers.

---

## Implicit Bias in Healthcare: The Evidence

| Population | Documented Disparity |
|------------|---------------------|
| **Black patients** | Undertreated for pain; less likely to receive mental health referrals |
| **Women** | Cardiac symptoms dismissed; more likely to receive psychiatric labels |
| **People with obesity** | Attributed symptoms to weight; less thorough workups |
| **LGBTQ+ patients** | Avoidance of care due to prior discrimination |
| **Patients with substance use disorders** | Labeled as "drug-seeking"; less empathy |
| **People with psychiatric diagnoses** | Physical symptoms attributed to mental illness ("diagnostic overshadowing") |

---

## How Implicit Bias Affects Nursing Practice

Implicit bias can subtly influence:
- **Pain assessment and management** — Who gets the full dose? Who is suspected of drug-seeking?
- **Therapeutic communication** — Who receives longer visits, more empathy?
- **Diagnostic reasoning** — Whose symptoms are taken seriously?
- **Discharge planning** — Whose social circumstances are explored?
- **Restraint use** — Who is perceived as threatening?

## Strategies to Mitigate Implicit Bias

### Individual Strategies
1. **Take the IAT** — Build self-awareness (implicit.harvard.edu)
2. **Slow down in decisions** — Bias is most influential when we're rushed
3. **Challenge your assumptions** — "Would I respond differently if this patient were different?"
4. **Perspective-taking** — Deliberately consider the patient's experience
5. **Reflect on language** — Audit your documentation for pejorative framing

### Structural Strategies
1. **Standardized assessment tools** — Use validated screening instruments
2. **Structured handoffs** — Reduce subjective "personality" information in patient reports
3. **Diverse teams** — Diverse clinical teams challenge in-group thinking
4. **Anti-bias training** — Regular, skills-based training (not just awareness)

---

## A Note on Systemic Racism in Healthcare

Individual implicit bias exists within larger **systems** that structurally disadvantage certain groups through:
- Differential access to insurance and care
- Underfunding of healthcare in low-income communities
- Historical abuses that create justified distrust (e.g., Tuskegee, forced sterilizations)
- Underrepresentation of diverse groups in medical research

Addressing health equity requires attention to *both* individual bias *and* systemic reform.

---

## Key Takeaways

- Implicit bias affects clinical care even among well-intentioned nurses
- Documented disparities exist across multiple patient populations and clinical domains
- Individual strategies (self-reflection, slowing down, IAT) complement structural approaches
- Addressing implicit bias is essential to achieving health equity
